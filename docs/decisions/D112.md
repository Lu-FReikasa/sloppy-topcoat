# D112 — A secretary is the first contact; reading tasks go out without a click

> **Status (2026-11-25):** Decided by the maintainer (plan of 2026-11-24, E5; confirmed 2026-11-25). Not built: phase P3 of that plan. Replaces [D31](D31.md) and amends [D65](D65.md).

**Decision.** A **secretary** (modelled on Jarvis) is the person's *first* contact, not the only one. The employees (Administration, Development, Research, Projects) stay directly reachable, and their contexts stay separate ([D74](D74.md)).

- **Routing by contract, not by words.** The secretary decides who does a task from the applications' contracts ([D118](D118.md)) and a routing schema, not from keyword patterns on the person's text.
- **Reading goes out without a click.** A task that only reads (look, search, research, summarise) is handed to the responsible application as a typed message ([D81](D81.md)) without a click. A task that changes state still needs the person's click or a scope ([D15](D15.md), [D83](D83.md)).
- **Results in two forms.** The employee answers with a `summary` for the person and a `brief` for the secretary; both pass the private-data filter of [D65](D65.md) before they enter the conversation. Files and Akten are passed by reference, never copied into the secretary's context; the secretary knows only what is not sensitive.
- **Watching on request.** The secretary keeps track of what it handed on, says the state when asked, and reports by itself when a task is done **only if the person asked for that**.
- **Direct conversation stays.** The person can always talk to an employee directly.

**Why.** The person asks for an outcome in one conversation ("why is the hub slow, can Development fix it, tell me when it is done") instead of clicking through several programs. Reading cannot change anything, so a click per reading task is friction without protection; changing stays behind the click. Steering by patterns on words (the old chat heuristic of D31) guessed from wording instead of from the task.
