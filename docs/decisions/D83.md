# D83 — Approvals follow the scope of the request, not a single attempt

**Decision.** **Approvals follow the scope of the request, not a single attempt (makes D15/D43 concrete a second time; decision D4-1 of the maintainer, 2026-10-29).** An approval names a *scope*: action kinds, target (repository and exact branch, or the named files), purpose and expiry. It is created only by the person's click on a card that shows exactly that scope, never by an API key or an agent, and it is revocable. Inside the scope, follow-up attempts need no new click: a failed attempt may be repeated, and a fix round on the approved branch is covered. A repeat is allowed after a technical failure or a correction that stays inside the scope; it is never made after a refusal of the checks (secret scan, size, force push, target outside the scope, expired or revoked approval) or after the same error twice; then the hub raises a question to the person. The hub decides from the *class of the error*, not from a model's opinion; the agent may ask for a retry and must say why. The checks that guard the action run on every attempt and are never part of what an approval can waive. Orders to Development and the push of the approved branch (a second entry point next to D43's click) are used this way; "approve all future changes" does not exist.

**Why.** The person already said what they want; asking again for each repair is friction, while an unbounded permission is not acceptable. A scope is bounded, visible and revocable, and the checks stay in code.
